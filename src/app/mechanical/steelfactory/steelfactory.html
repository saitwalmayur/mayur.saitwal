AIST & Purdue CIVS Steel Manufacturing

Hot Rolled Strip Steel Manufacturing Process

STAGE 1

Slab Reheating (1250°C)

Cast steel slabs (280mm thick, ~25 tonnes) enter a natural-gas fired walking beam furnace. Slabs are uniformly heated to 1250°C to achieve full austenite homogenization and optimal plasticity for rolling.

STAGE 2

Primary High-Pressure Descaling

As the glowing slab discharges, high-pressure water jets (300–350 bar) blast both top and bottom surfaces, peeling off iron oxide mill scale to guarantee clean roll bite without surface defects.

STAGE 3

Roughing Mill & Edging (4-High Reversing)

Massive 4-high work rolls and vertical edgers break down the cast dendritic structure, reducing slab thickness from 280mm down to a 35–45mm transfer bar while elongating its length.

STAGE 4

Tandem Finishing Mill Train (F1 to F6)

Six synchronized 4-high stands roll the continuous strip at accelerating speeds up to 15 m/s, precisely reducing thickness down to final customer gauge (1.5mm to 6.0mm).

STAGE 5

Laminar Flow Quenching Run-Out Table

Overhead U-tube laminar cooling headers pour curtains of water to rapidly quench the strip from 900°C to 550°C, locking in targeted ferrite-pearlite grain size and mechanical tensile strength.

STAGE 6

Downcoiler, Strapping & Coil Discharge

High-speed pinch rolls feed the strip into the underground downcoiler. Expanding mandrel and hydraulic wrapper rolls wind the ribbon into a tight Hot Rolled Coil (HRC), strapped with steel bands and discharged via coil car.
